## Milestones

Source for every bullet: [CDC Learn the Signs. Act Early.](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/index.html), with the age page linked on each heading. Bullets are paraphrased.

**2 months** ([source](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-months.html))
- Calms down when spoken to or picked up.
- Looks at your face and smiles when you talk or smile.
- Makes sounds other than crying and reacts to loud sounds.
- Holds head up on tummy and opens hands briefly.

**4 months** ([source](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-months.html))
- Smiles on purpose and chuckles to get attention.
- Coos and makes sounds back when you talk.
- Turns head toward the sound of your voice.
- Holds head steady and pushes up onto elbows on tummy.

**6 months** ([source](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/6-months.html))
- Knows familiar people and laughs.
- Takes turns making sounds with you.
- Rolls from tummy to back.
- Leans on hands to sit.

**9 months** ([source](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/9-months.html))
- Shows stranger wariness and reacts when you leave.
- Smiles or laughs at peek-a-boo.
- Gets to a sitting position alone and passes objects between hands.
- Sits without support.

**12 months** ([source](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/1-year.html))
- Plays games like pat-a-cake.
- Waves bye-bye and says mama or dada (or another name).
- Understands "no".
- Pulls up to stand and walks holding furniture.

**15 months** ([source](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/15-months.html))
- Tries to say one or two words besides mama or dada.
- Points to ask for something or get help.
- Follows directions given with a gesture and words.
- Takes a few steps alone.

**18 months** ([source](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/18-months.html))
- Tries to say three or more words.
- Follows one-step directions without gestures.
- Walks without holding on to anything.
- Scribbles and feeds self with fingers.

**24 months** ([source](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-years.html))
- Says at least two words together.
- Points to things in a book and to body parts when asked.
- Notices when others are hurt or upset.
- Kicks a ball, runs, and walks up a few stairs.

## Conflicts and changes

Vaccine schedule, CDC vs AAP. The CDC schedule page opened here is the July 2, 2025 version ([CDC schedule](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/child-adolescent-age.html)). CRS reports a CDC revision issued January 5, 2026. It moved the birth dose for infants of HBsAg-negative mothers to shared decision-making, and moved the first RSV dose for infants under 8 months not protected by maternal vaccination from the all-children group to a high-risk group ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12684.html)). CRS reports a March 16, 2026 court stay that postponed the 2026 schedule and reverted to the January 2025 versions. Contemporary Pediatrics instead reports that the stay reinstated the May 2025 version, and that the AAP left its RSV recommendation unchanged (verify which version and RSV status apply) ([Contemporary Pediatrics](https://www.contemporarypediatrics.com/view/the-unharmonious-2026-child-and-adolescent-immunization-schedule)). The AAP rejected the CDC 2026 changes and endorses the 2025 harmonized schedule. Under the CDC 2026 version, rotavirus, influenza, COVID-19 and hepatitis A move to shared decision-making (same source). The AAP 2026 schedule page opened but has no vaccine table ([AAP](https://www.aap.org/link/9ef7b3834e984107812cb7f8e8e96194.aspx)), so AAP dose ages in this file are (verify).

Lead screening. Bright Futures evidence recommends a blood lead test at 12 months and says it may be repeated at 24 months ([Bright Futures evidence](https://downloads.aap.org/AAP/PDF/Bright%20Futures/BF4_Evidence_Rationale.pdf)). The same document says USPSTF recommends against routine screening of asymptomatic average-risk children aged 1 to 5 (grade D). The Bright Futures schedule marks lead as "to be performed" or "risk assessment" by visit and footnote 26 ties testing to Medicaid or high-prevalence rules. Show the risk-based path and the state rule, not a universal test.

Newborn visits. research.md lists visits at 3 to 5 days, 2 weeks and 1 month. The Bright Futures schedule has a 3 to 5 day visit, then 1 month, and no 2 week visit ([Bright Futures schedule](https://downloads.aap.org/aap/pdf/periodicity_schedule.pdf)). Remove the 2 week visit unless a source for it is found.

Screen time under 2 years. The AAP 2026 policy replaced fixed limits. The AAP page opened gives no age windows for under 2 or 2 to 5 years ([AAP](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/understanding-the-new-aap-digital-media-guidelines/)). The "avoid screens under 18 months" rule comes only from secondary sources, so it stays (verify) until the policy statement is read.

Screening tools. The Bright Futures schedule names the ages (9, 18 and 30 months for developmental screening; 18 and 24 months for autism screening) but not the tools. research.md names ASQ and M-CHAT. Confirm the tool names against AAP's own text before the rules engine shows them.

Peanut introduction. research.md gives 4 to 6 months for high-risk infants. The NIAID addendum tiers the start age by eczema and egg allergy status (verify on the NIAID page; not opened).

Bright Futures edition. The periodicity schedule is the February 2025 update of the 4th edition guidelines. The evidence rationale is the 4th edition document ([Bright Futures schedule](https://downloads.aap.org/aap/pdf/periodicity_schedule.pdf); [Bright Futures evidence](https://downloads.aap.org/AAP/PDF/Bright%20Futures/BF4_Evidence_Rationale.pdf)).
