# Child, 2 to 9 years: what to do when

Compiled 2026-11-14. Extends `docs/research.md` (toddler, preschool and school-age stages). Rows marked "(verify)" cite a page that could not be opened in this pass; check the page before the rule goes into app logic. "Checked" is the date the cited page was opened.

## Conflicts and changes

- **Blood pressure, 3 years and up.** Bright Futures schedules measurement at each well visit from age 3 ([Bright Futures](https://downloads.aap.org/AAP/PDF/periodicity_schedule.pdf)). The USPSTF gives grade I (2020), meaning evidence is insufficient to recommend for or against screening ([USPSTF BP](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/blood-pressure-in-children-and-adolescents-hypertension-screening)). The app should cite both and say the AAP schedule is the one it follows.
- **Vision, 1 to 5 years.** USPSTF grade B (2017) says screen at least once at 3 to 5 years. The same page says instrument-based screening may be used at ages 1 to 3 when available, citing a 2016 joint report, and that the topic is being updated ([USPSTF vision](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/vision-in-children-ages-6-months-to-5-years-screening)). Bright Futures uses instrument screening at 12 and 24 months and acuity at 3 to 5 years ([Bright Futures](https://downloads.aap.org/AAP/PDF/periodicity_schedule.pdf) fn 7).
- **Autism screening, 18 and 24 months.** Bright Futures makes it universal. USPSTF gives grade I (2016), meaning insufficient evidence ([USPSTF autism](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/autism-spectrum-disorder-in-young-children-screening)).
- **Lipid screen, 9 to 11 years.** Bright Futures and the AAP periodicity page schedule it once. USPSTF gives grade I (2023) with no fixed ages ([USPSTF lipid](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/lipid-disorders-in-children-screening)).
- **Anxiety screening.** USPSTF grade B from age 8. Bright Futures does not list a formal anxiety screen at 6 or 7, only the behavioral screen ([USPSTF anxiety](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-anxiety-children-adolescents)).
- **Booster exit age.** CDC says the adult seat belt usually fits at ages 9 to 12 ([CDC booster guide](https://www.cdc.gov/child-passenger-safety/media/pdfs/Booster-Seat-Guide_508c.pdf)). HealthyChildren gives 8 to 12 with 4 ft 9 in (verify, not opened). The task brief said 8 to 12. Use height as the decision rule and state the age range that applies.
- **Vaccine schedules.** The CDC announced a revised 2026 childhood schedule on January 5, 2026. The U.S. District Court for Massachusetts stayed it on March 16, 2026 (AAP v. Kennedy), and CDC schedules mostly revert to January 2025 versions ([CRS IN12684](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12684.html)). The CDC page opened here is the July 2, 2025 addendum ([CDC schedule](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/child-adolescent-age.html)). The AAP 2026 schedule is marked updated September 2, 2026 ([AAP 2026 schedule PDF](https://downloads.aap.org/AAP/PDF/AAP-Immunization-Schedule.pdf)). For ages 4 to 6, both list DTaP fifth, IPV fourth, MMR second and varicella second.
- **Influenza.** AAP recommends yearly vaccination from 6 months, two doses in the first season under 9 ([AAP flu 2026-27](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/influenza/preparing-for-flu-season/)). A secondary source says the CDC has not released 2026-27 flu recommendations (verify, not opened).
- **Screen time.** The AAP's January 20, 2026 policy statement moved away from fixed clock limits ([AAP digital media guidance](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/understanding-the-new-AAP-digital-media-guidelines/)). AAP's parent Q&A still says under one hour a day for toddlers and preschoolers ([AAP Q&A](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/qa-portal/qa-portal-library/qa-portal-library-questions/reducing-screen-time-for-toddlers-cold-turkey-or-gradual-change/)). The app must show which applies and date it.
- **Sealants.** The AAPD says coverage should follow caries risk, not age, and that eruption timing varies ([AAPD sealant policy](https://www.aapd.org/globalassets/media/policies_guidelines/p_3rdreimbursementsealants26.pdf)). The brief's "around 6" is an eruption marker, not an AAPD age rule.
- **Dental home timing (for research.md).** research.md says "dental home by age 1 (verify AAPD)". The AAPD parent FAQ confirms the first visit by the first birthday ([AAPD FAQ](https://www.aapd.org/resources/parent/faq/)). Update research.md.
- **Lead testing.** Bright Futures says risk assessment at 3 to 6 years, testing if at risk. CDC's Medicaid rule is 12 and 24 months, or 24 to 72 months if never tested ([CDC lead](https://www.cdc.gov/lead-prevention/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html)). The CDC page does not state the numeric reference value; check the CDC lead data page before writing it into the app.
- **Activity (for research.md).** research.md says "60 minutes of activity a day" for ages 5 to 9. HHS sets 60 minutes for ages 6 to 17 and "active throughout the day" for ages 3 to 5 ([HHS](https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/physical-activity/current-guidelines/top-10-things-know)). Update research.md.
- **Gaps not covered by an opened source.** Preschool enrollment timing, toilet training age window (verify), ADHD details (verify), autism follow-up (verify), body safety (verify), swim-lesson start age (verify), 4 ft 9 in booster height (verify).
