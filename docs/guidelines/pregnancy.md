# Pregnancy: what to do when

Compiled on 2026-11-14. Each row cites a page that was opened; rows marked (verify) cite the closest page that was opened, because the primary ACOG page could not be opened.

| Before trying | Keep up routine dental checkups and tell the dentist if you might be pregnant. | Mother | [NICHD: Healthy Pregnancy](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/preconceptioncare/conditioninfo/healthy-pregnancy) | 2026-11-14 |

## Weeks 4 to 13

| When | What to do | Who | Source | Checked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Early prenatal visit, in every pregnancy | Get a hepatitis B surface antigen test in each pregnancy, even if you were tested before. | Mother | [CDC: Hepatitis B Clinical Overview](https://www.cdc.gov/hepatitis-b/hcp/clinical-overview/index.html) | 2026-11-14 |

| Throughout pregnancy | Avoid raw fish, undercooked meat, deli meat and unpasteurized cheese. | Mother | [NICHD: Healthy Pregnancy](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/preconceptioncare/conditioninfo/healthy-pregnancy) | 2026-11-14 |
| First visit | Ask about iron-deficiency anemia screening and a B12 supplement if you are vegan, as your provider advises. | Mother | [NICHD: Healthy Pregnancy](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/preconceptioncare/conditioninfo/healthy-pregnancy) | 2026-11-14 |

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Conflicts and changes

- COVID-19 in pregnancy. ACOG's 2026 schedule (June 2026) says all pregnant people should get COVID-19 vaccine at any gestational age ([ACOG 2026 schedule](https://cmss.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/ACOG-2026-Maternal-Immunization-Schedule.pdf)). CDC's sources disagree. The CDC guidelines page (dated Aug 22, 2025) lists COVID-19 as "no guidance/not applicable" in pregnancy ([CDC: Guidelines for Vaccinating Pregnant Women](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines-pregnancy/hcp/vaccination-guidelines/index.html)). The CDC hub page still lists COVID-19 among vaccines recommended before or during pregnancy ([CDC: Vaccines for Pregnant Women](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines-pregnancy/hcp/index.html)). CIDRAP reports that CDC dropped its pregnancy recommendation "last May" ([CIDRAP](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/influenza-vaccines/leading-ob-gyn-society-breaks-cdc-recommended-vaccines-pregnant-women-including)). Immunize.org says CDC's September 2026 clinical considerations removed safety and benefit text, while CDC still routinely recommends COVID-19 vaccine for adults, including pregnant people ([Immunize.org](https://www.immunize.org/ask-experts/are-covid-19-vaccines-recommended-during-pregnancy)). The app should state the ACOG position and flag the CDC status as unresolved until someone reads the current CDC page.
- RSV season end. ACOG's 2026 schedule and CDC's RSV page both say September through January ([ACOG 2026 schedule](https://cmss.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/ACOG-2026-Maternal-Immunization-Schedule.pdf); [CDC RSV](https://www.cdc.gov/rsv/hcp/vaccine-clinical-guidance/pregnant-people.html)). A September 2026 report says ACOG's 2026-27 guidance extends the window to March 1 and gives 32 0/7 to 36 6/7 weeks, excluding deliveries planned within about 2 weeks ([Contemporary OB/GYN](https://www.contemporaryobgyn.net/view/acog-2026-27-respiratory-virus-immunization-recommendations-pregnancy)). The ACOG PDF I opened still says January. Check the current ACOG text before the app uses March 1 (verify).
- Folic acid dose. USPSTF gives 400 to 800 mcg for anyone who could become pregnant and excludes people with a prior neural tube defect pregnancy ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/folic-acid-for-the-prevention-of-neural-tube-defects-preventive-medication)). CDC gives 400 mcg ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/folic-acid/about/index.html)). NICHD gives 400 mcg and suggests 4 mg after a prior neural tube defect ([NICHD](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/preconceptioncare/conditioninfo/healthy-pregnancy)). The research.md preconception line uses the USPSTF range, so the 4 mg case needs its own rule.
- Low-dose aspirin. USPSTF (Sept 2021) says 81 mg daily after week 12 for people with a high-risk factor ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/low-dose-aspirin-use-for-the-prevention-of-morbidity-and-mortality-from-preeclampsia-preventive-medication)). The ACOG Committee Opinion 743 record describes a start between 12 and 28 weeks, ideally before 16 weeks, with daily use until delivery, and a search summary says ACOG and SMFM later moved to a risk-based approach ([PubMed: ACOG CO 743](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29939940/)) (verify). Use the USPSTF rule until the ACOG text is read.
- Prenatal visit schedule. NICHD's fixed schedule (monthly to 28 weeks, every 2 weeks to 36, then weekly) matches research.md. ACOG's April 2025 guidance, seen only in search summaries, describes a tailored, risk-based schedule with fewer routine visits for low-risk pregnancies (verify on acog.org). The app should treat the fixed schedule as the default and allow a risk-based alternative.
- Flu timing. CDC's guidelines page prefers September or October in trimesters 1 and 2 and advises against July and August ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines-pregnancy/hcp/vaccination-guidelines/index.html)). ACOG's 2026 schedule says flu vaccine can be given in any trimester, at any time while the virus circulates ([ACOG 2026 schedule](https://cmss.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/ACOG-2026-Maternal-Immunization-Schedule.pdf)).
- Glucose screening window. research.md says 24 to 28 weeks. The USPSTF recommendation has no upper limit: screening at 24 weeks or after ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/gestational-diabetes-screening)). The app rule should use "24 weeks or later" unless ACOG's 24 to 28 window is read and confirmed.
- Group B strep window. CDC's current page says 36 or 37 weeks, following ACOG and ACNM ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/group-b-strep/testing/index.html)). Older CDC patient materials used 35 to 37 weeks (search summary only, verify). research.md's 36 to 37 window matches the current page.
- Hepatitis B birth dose. CDC's hepatitis B clinical overview (reviewed Nov 2, 2026) lists a September 18, 2025 ACIP birth dose briefing, but the opened text does not state the birth-dose recommendation for infants of mothers who are HBsAg-positive or unknown ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/hepatitis-b/hcp/clinical-overview/index.html)). Treat the infant birth dose as in flux.
- Asymptomatic bacteriuria grade. The USPSTF recommendation page and clinician summary both say grade B (2019). A search summary of the same page said grade A since 1996; the opened page does not support that, so keep B.

## Gaps not filled

1. Carrier screening: the ACOG Committee Opinion (CO 690) could not be opened (ACOG returned 402), and no other opened page covers it. Research.md's "verify ACOG CO 690" is still open.
2. Dating ultrasound window, anatomy scan window (research.md says 18 to 22 weeks, but no opened page confirmed it), and when to begin kick counting (no week given on the NICHD page).
3. Pediatrician choice timing, car seat installation, hospital bag, childbirth classes, and induction timing at 39 and 41 weeks (ACOG CO 831 and CO 764) could not be sourced: HealthyChildren.org and NHTSA returned 403 or 404, and no ACOG page could be opened.
